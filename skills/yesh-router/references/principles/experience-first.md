# Experience first

**Read when:** product, UX, or feature-scope tradeoffs come up.

- The product is the experience; every technical decision either helps or hurts it. When implementation convenience conflicts with user delight, choose delight.
- Say no: every feature, control, and option must earn its place. Ship fewer polished features over more rough ones.
- Sweat the details: transitions, alignment, spacing, feedback, and error states.
- Look at a UI yourself before showing it: its screenshot in light and dark, beside the user's reference when there is one. A page that renders is not one that looks right.
- The user is whoever consumes the work, as [design-from-usage](design-from-usage.md) defines; weigh their experience the same way.
- Tighten the core loop: every feature serves the central workflow or gets out of the way.

**Limits:** this governs the target of the work; the sequence of work is governed by other notes. Foundational structure choices are [redesign-from-first-principles](redesign-from-first-principles.md).
